<div class="blog-text" markdown="1">

<div class="drop-cap">
Multi-Agent Systems have always been my primary research interest and, more importantly, an underlying source of curiosity that drives my scientific journey. The more I study and reflect on this field, the more I feel that its deepest questions should never be confined to computer science alone.
</div>

After all, multi-agent systems are not merely about making multiple intelligent agents work together. They lead us to a much more fundamental question: **How can independent individuals, through their interactions, give rise to complex collective behavior?**

I love **economics** because my background in game theory and mechanism design has given me a powerful perspective on understanding systems. These disciplines have taught me to think about how rules, incentives, and strategic interactions shape the behavior of entire collectives. Sometimes, changing a simple rule can fundamentally transform how a system behaves.

I am equally fascinated by **physics and biology**, although for a somewhat different reason.

I sometimes feel as though the Creator has already written the most sophisticated forms of collective intelligence into the natural world. Perhaps we humans have only begun to understand these designs, let alone translate their underlying principles into artificial intelligent systems.

Recently, I built a physics-inspired multi-agent simulator, where numerous particles move, collide, and interact according to simple local rules. I wanted to observe how collective patterns might emerge from these seemingly elementary interactions.

I also enjoy reading biological research on ant colonies. Different ant species have developed remarkably intricate mechanisms for communication, cooperation, and collective decision-making. What fascinates me is how such small individuals, without necessarily possessing a global understanding of their environment, can collectively produce such sophisticated behavior.

Over time, I have come to realize that economics, physics, and biology are all gradually shaping the way I understand multi-agent systems.

<div class="custom-quote">
  "<strong>How can simple local interactions give rise to complex collective intelligence?</strong>"
</div>

It is a question that seems to connect these different disciplines, despite their very different languages and methodologies.

Sometimes, I find myself simply staring at these moving particles, almost mesmerized.

They approach one another, collide, separate, and reorganize. Each particle follows relatively simple rules, yet their interactions can produce surprisingly intricate patterns.

There is something profoundly beautiful about watching complexity emerge from simplicity.

**Why can simple rules produce such complex order? How can individuals with no knowledge of the entire system collectively create something that resembles intelligence?**

I do not pretend to have all the answers. But perhaps the beauty of research lies precisely in pursuing questions that continue to surprise us.

The more I think about these questions, the more I feel that multi-agent systems, at least to me, are becoming something beyond a field of computer science.

They are becoming a kind of **philosophy**: a way of understanding individuals, collectives, interactions, institutions, and emergence.

And perhaps philosophy is, in some sense, at the heart of everything. It begins whenever we try to understand the deeper principles behind the world around us.

</div>
