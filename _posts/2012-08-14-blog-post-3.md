Multi-agent systems have always been my primary research interest and a fundamental source of curiosity in my scientific journey. The more I reflect on this field, the more I feel that it should never be confined to computer science alone.

I love **economics** because studying game theory and mechanism design has given me a perspective on how rules, incentives, and interactions shape the behavior of an entire system.

I also love **physics and biology**, because I feel that the Creator has already written the most sophisticated forms of collective intelligence into the natural world. We humans simply need to understand them better and learn how to apply their principles. So recently, I built a simulator inspired by the collisions and interactions of physical particles, just to observe how they interact. I also enjoy reading biological research on ant colonies, as I find the ways different ant species cooperate and communicate truly fascinating.

I've come to realize that these different disciplines are gradually shaping my understanding of multi-agent systems.

Sometimes, I find myself staring at these interacting particles in my simulator, completely mesmerized. They are so intricate and beautiful. Watching collective intelligence emerge from their interactions is simply incredible.

So I've gradually come to feel that multi-agent systems are, in a sense, a kind of **philosophy**, because philosophy is everything.

Perhaps I could call this simulator **"A Visualization of Philosophy."**
